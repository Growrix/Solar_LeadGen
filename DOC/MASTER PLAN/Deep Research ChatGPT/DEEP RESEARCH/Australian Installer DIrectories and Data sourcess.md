Australian Installer Directories and Data Sources
Australia has numerous online directories and marketplaces that list installers (solar, electrical, plumbing, etc.) along with contact details (company name, address, phone). Major general business directories like Yellow Pages, TrueLocal, AussieWeb and Hotfrog cover all trade categories[1][2]. For example, YellowPages (aussieweb.com.au/yellowpages) and TrueLocal (truelocal.com.au) are among the top national directories[1][2]. TrueLocal advertises itself as “Australia’s largest collection of trusted local businesses” with categories for plumbers, electricians, builders, etc.[2]. Similarly, directory sites like AussieWeb, Yelp Australia and Hotfrog list thousands of businesses (including installers) nationwide[1]. These sites generally allow filtering by service type and location.
Trade-Specific Listing Platforms
In addition to general directories, there are trade-specific platforms and marketplaces. For example, trade-focused directories and lead sites are popular with installers: hipages, Oneflare, Airtasker, ServiceSeeking, TradieWives, YourTradie, Search A Tradie, etc.[3]. (Many of these require businesses to register or pay for premium listings.) An industry blog lists hipages, Airtasker, ServiceSeeking, Oneflare and others as key trade directories in Australia[3]. For instance, hipages and Airtasker match customers to tradies nationwide, while Oneflare lets users post a job and get quotes from local installers. These platforms typically display company name, area served, and often phone/contact info.
For illustration, Oneflare’s listing pages show top electricians by city. The “Best electricians in Sydney, NSW” page on Oneflare (powered by Airtasker) shows electrician names, locations and partially visible contact numbers[4]. Likewise, the “Best electricians in Melbourne, VIC” page lists Melbourne-based electricians[5]. (These pages require interaction to view full details, but they demonstrate the breadth of listings by location.) Such marketplaces can be searched or filtered by postcode/region to gather NSW or VIC installers.
Plumbing and electrical trades also have dedicated directories. Plumbers Today Directory (plumberstoday.com.au) advertises a “trusted directory of licensed and verified plumbers” across major cities[6]. For example, its Sydney page lists companies like Hello Plumbing with phone and suburb (“Hello Plumbing… 02 8320 8888”)[7]. The directory explicitly highlights coverage of “licensed plumbers in major Australian cities including Sydney, Melbourne, Brisbane…”[6]. Similarly, the Master Plumbers Association (mpav.com.au) and Master Electricians Australia (masterelectricians.com.au) offer member search tools (some with login restrictions).
For solar installers, specialized directories exist. One example is Solar Directory (solardirectory.com.au), titled “Australian Solar and Battery Installers Directory”[8]. This site lists solar/battery companies by name and location, with profile pages containing address and phone. For instance, the listing for “Todd’s Plumbing and Solar” shows its Gold Coast address and phone number[9]. (Solar Directory also provides ABN/licensing details and website links on each profile.) Another approach is to use the Australian government’s ABN Lookup or Energy website: the federal energy site suggests consumers “conduct your own online search for installers in your area” and verify the installer’s accreditation[10]. Note that Clean Energy Council accreditation moved to Solar Accreditation Australia, so one must often verify accreditation via SAAustralia or CEC membership.
State and Regulatory Resources
Many state governments provide licensing/search tools, though these typically verify credentials rather than list all installers. For NSW, Service NSW offers a “Check a builder or tradesperson licence” tool[11]. You can enter a name, licence number or ABN to confirm validity – this is useful for verification but isn’t a bulk directory. Similarly, in Victoria Energy Safe Victoria maintains public registers for electrical contractors[12], enabling search by name or licence (with CAPTCHA). The Victorian Building Authority also has a “Find a practitioner” search for plumbers and builders, though it’s geared to license status rather than listing contact details. These government sites ensure the tradesperson is licensed in NSW or VIC but do not readily provide email or phone.
Key Directory Sites (Examples)
Yellow Pages (yellowpages.com.au) – Australia’s traditional directory, searchable by trade and location[1].
TrueLocal (truelocal.com.au) – NewsCorp-owned local business directory; has categories for plumbers, electricians, etc.[2]
AussieWeb (aussieweb.com.au) – Long-running business directory, often cited as a top directory[1].
Hotfrog (hotfrog.com.au) – Free global business directory with Australian listings.
Yelp Australia (yelp.com.au) – User-review site with business listings (including trades).
Bing/Google Business – Not a single site to scrape, but search engines aggregate local listings (for background).
PureLocal (purelocal.com.au) – Australian online directory (free and premium listings).
hipages (hipages.com.au) – Trade-lead platform.
Oneflare (oneflare.com.au) – Quote-request marketplace; has many electrician/plumber listings.
Airtasker (airtasker.com) – General task platform; plumbers and electricians often advertise here (e.g. Airtasker’s “Find an electrician near you” pages).
ServiceSeeking (serviceseeking.com.au) – Quote marketplace for tradies.
TradieWives, YourTradie, Search A Tradie, Tradie Centre – Smaller local tradie directories[3].
Each of these sites can be searched or browsed by trade and region. For instance, using “plumber” or “electrician” plus state or city filters on YellowPages or TrueLocal yields extensive lists with addresses and phone numbers. Plumbers Today Directory (as shown above) provides a cleaner list of NSW and VIC plumbers with contact info[7][6]. Oneflare and Airtasker listings (e.g. for Sydney or Melbourne) similarly show names and phone snippets[5][4].
Regional Focus: NSW vs VIC
NSW: In addition to national directories, NSW installers can be found via Sydney-area sections on major sites. For example, TrueLocal’s Sydney plumbers/electricians categories list thousands of providers[2]. Plumbers Today covers Sydney and NSW (see Hello Plumbing example)[7]. Airtasker/Oneflare pages like “Best electricians in Sydney” list Sydney-based electricians by name with partial contact[4]. Use the Service NSW licence check[11] to verify any NSW electrician, plumber or builder by name/ABN.
VIC: Similarly, directories cover Melbourne and Victoria. Oneflare’s “Best electricians in Melbourne” page lists VIC electricians[5]. Plumbers Today has a Melbourne plumbers section. Energy Safe Victoria’s search can verify VIC electrical contractors[12]. The Master Plumbers Association of Victoria also has a “Find a plumber” registry (for licensed plumbers in VIC), though it may require login. In both NSW and VIC, using YellowPages/TrueLocal with state filters will yield large lists of installers with addresses and phones.
Important: Collecting data at scale may violate some sites’ terms of use or robots.txt. Always check each directory’s usage rules. Where possible, use official APIs or request permission. The sources above (especially phone and email) are publicly displayed on the businesses’ own listings, but automated scraping may still be restricted. For reliable data, consider combining multiple sources (e.g. cross-check ABN details on abr.business.gov.au, use business websites) as directories often update. By systematically querying these directories (YellowPages, TrueLocal, trade sites like Hipages/Oneflare, and state registers), one can compile a comprehensive list of installers in NSW and VIC.
